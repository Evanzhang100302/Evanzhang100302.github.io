About

I’m a Ph.D. student in Statistics at the University of California, Riverside, advised by Prof. Yuzhou Chen. I study generative models for spatio-temporal event data: diffusion-based point processes that draw on satellite imagery and on topological structure from discrete Morse theory, and distillation methods that make these models fast enough to use in practice. Most of my applications are in AI for Earth and public safety, especially wildfire and smoke forecasting from multi-source satellite observations.

Before UCR, I earned a Master of Applied Statistics at The Ohio State University and a B.Eng. in Microelectronic Science and Engineering at Xi’an Jiaotong University.

Publications

  1. NeurIPS 2026
    Poster

    MCM-DM: Towards Better Spatio-Temporal Event Representation Learning via Discrete Morse Theory

    Yuanheng Zhang, Chenguang Yang, Jaidev Goel, Jennifer Rozenblit, Yulia R. Gel, Yuzhou Chen

    A cross-modality diffusion framework for spatio-temporal point processes. It encodes satellite imagery with a vision–language model and uses discrete Morse theory to align event representations with the topology of the data, evaluated on eight real-world datasets.

  2. SIGSPATIAL 2026
    Poster

    Uncertainty-aware Multi-modality Spatio-temporal Wildfire and Smoke Prediction

    Yuanheng Zhang, Chenguang Yang, Nicholas LaHaye, Huikyo Lee, Yulia R. Gel, Yuzhou Chen

    UMS predicts wildfire and smoke from multi-source satellite and airborne imagery (GOES-17/18, GK2A, MASTER, eMAS), pairing mutual k-NN graphs with a mixture-of-experts design for uncertainty estimates.
